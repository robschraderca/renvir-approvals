The pattern breaks on Sept 15. Before that the store saw one or two checkout starts on a good day. From Sept 15 it saw ten to twenty, and across the five days to Sept 19, sixty six sessions reached checkout and none completed.

The checkout is not dead. Two orders completed through the live site on Sept 20 and 21. Whatever stops people, it is not a broken payment step.

The strongest candidate is the offer link. Supercharger plus Alpha Detox is served as a cart permalink priced at $44.90 and it is not configured properly. Two things point at it, neither conclusive. The ad account records add to cart at 167% of link clicks on the Facebook feed ad carrying the offer, which is not possible through ordinary clicking. And all nine abandoned bundle carts arrive holding the same three items with the discount already applied, which is what a pre-loaded cart link produces.

The abandoned carts

Fifteen checkouts were started and never finished, worth $574.98, running Sept 4 to 21. The store's entire lifetime revenue is $658.38, so eighteen days of abandoned carts is worth almost as much as every order ever placed. Shopify only keeps a record once contact details are left, so fifteen is a floor.

Nine of the fifteen are the identical cart. Citrus Revive, Supercharger and Alpha Detox at $44.90, all United States addresses, all between Sept 18 and 21: South Carolina, Florida, New Jersey twice, Georgia, Texas, Colorado and New York.

These are real people and the offer is working on them. Eight of the nine got as far as entering a shipping address, which is past the cart and past the contact step. They stopped after that. Two tried more than once, one of them twice inside a minute. Someone who retries that fast is hitting something, not deciding the price is too high.

What that rules out

Mobile produced 76 checkout sessions and 3 purchases, desktop 31 and 1, so both fail at about the same rate and the checkout is not broken on phones. United States traffic produced 99 of the 111 checkout sessions and all four purchases, so geography is roughly a tenth of the problem. Shipping is configured, and every completed order since Sept 14 used free standard shipping. That pushes payment to the front of the queue, and payment is the one step this data cannot see.

Everything that tags cleanly is set to Continue. Deny with nothing on hand is what a product feed typically reports as out of stock, and Meta does not allow an out of stock item to be tagged. That is the likely mechanism rather than a confirmed one, because the Meta catalog cannot be inspected from the store side.

Depuff and Revive cannot be bought. Added Sept 18, it tracks inventory, holds zero units and refuses sales at zero. That combination blocks checkout outright, and it is published only to the online store, so it cannot be tagged or advertised either. Anything pointing at it is spending against a product nobody can buy.

The pricing does not line up

The $44.90 link is Alpha Detox at $19.95 plus Supercharger at $24.95, both at full list price, so it charges $4.95 more than the store's own kit for the same two products. With the free serum attached it hands over $72.85 of product for $44.90, while the Complete Face Regimen is those same three products for $59.95. Every redemption undercuts the store's own three step bundle by $15.05. It has been redeemed twice, which are the two internal orders. No discount code in the store's history has ever been redeemed.

What to do

  1. Reprice the Complete Face Regimen to $44.90, with a compare at price of $72.85. It holds the same three products the offer gives away and it is the only bundle already configured correctly: active, set to Continue, and live on all five channels including Facebook and Instagram. One price field turns the offer into a real product with a real URL that can be tagged, advertised and reported on. The relaunch site already puts the Complete Face Regimen forward as the system, so this moves with where the site is going.
  2. Unpublish the two product kits while the offer runs. At $44.90 for three products, Race Day at $44.95 and Revive Hydration Duo at $47.95 both cost more than the bundle that contains them. None has sold more than once.
  3. Repoint every ad at the regimen product page and retire the permalink. This also fixes the measurement. Traffic that lands on a product page and adds to cart produces an honest add to cart number, which the permalink never did.
  4. Fix Depuff and Revive or stop pointing anything at it.
  5. Strip the internal orders out of every September number. Meta is currently reporting the internal test order as the campaign's only conversion, so any cost per purchase built on it is wrong in the flattering direction.
  6. Walk the checkout as a stranger. On a phone, from an ad click, with no code, to the payment screen. Ten minutes, and it turns the payment theory into a fact or kills it.
  7. Work the nine abandoned carts. Shopify holds a recovery URL and a customer record for each, and Klaviyo is already installed.

Items 1, 2 and 4 change the store, and access on this side is read only by agreement, so those three need someone with write access.

What could still move these numbers

The site measured here is being replaced. Every funnel number above describes renvirco.com as it stands today, and that version is on its way out. The relaunch theme was still being worked on at 06:38 UTC on Sept 22. It carries a 90 day money-back guarantee where the live site carries a 60 day promise and never mentions money back, so any ad claim has to match whichever site is live on the day it runs. It is built on Never Stop, it puts the Complete Face Regimen forward as the system, and it gives Depuff and Revive a section with an empty video slot. Treat the figures above as the baseline the relaunch has to beat, not as a description of what customers will meet next week.

Amazon, and it is no longer hypothetical. The ad account holds campaigns named for it. One carries $242.78 of spend and 1,864 link clicks, the largest single line in the account's history, and there is a second. Paid traffic was deliberately sent to Amazon rather than to the store, which is part of why the store's own numbers read as thin as they do. Nothing that happened after those clicks appears here. Every figure describes the Shopify store, not the business, and only Amazon's own reporting will close that gap.

Attribution leaking into direct. Of 870 sessions across sixty days, 393 are logged as direct. Some unknown share are ad clicks that lost their referrer to privacy settings, ad blockers or stripped link parameters. Social's contribution is therefore understated and direct's overstated, which makes the flat claim that social produced no purchases softer than it reads.

Klaviyo. Installed on the store and not readable from here. Whether its abandoned cart flow is switched on decides whether those fifteen carts are already being worked or sitting untouched.

The payment step. Shopify does not expose why a payment failed, or which gateways are live and in what mode. Why those nine stopped is the one question this data cannot answer.
